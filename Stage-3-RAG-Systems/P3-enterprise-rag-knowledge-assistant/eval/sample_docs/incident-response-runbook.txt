Incident Response Runbook
Version 4.1 | Maintained by: Engineering Operations | Last Updated: 2024-01-10

1. PURPOSE AND SCOPE

This runbook defines the end-to-end incident response process for all production
services. It covers detection, triage, escalation, resolution, and post-incident
review procedures. All on-call engineers are required to follow this runbook.

2. INCIDENT SEVERITY LEVELS

P1 - Critical: Complete service outage or data loss risk. Response within 15 minutes.
P2 - High: Major feature degradation affecting more than 20% of users. Response within 1 hour.
P3 - Medium: Partial degradation, workaround available. Response within 4 hours.
P4 - Low: Minor issues, no customer impact. Response next business day.

3. INCIDENT RESPONSE WORKFLOW

3.1 Detection and Declaration
Incidents are detected via automated alerting or customer reports. The on-call engineer
declares an incident by creating an incident ticket in the incident management system
and posting in the #incidents Slack channel.

3.2 Standard Change Management Integration
In normal operations, all infrastructure and configuration changes must go through the
standard Change Advisory Board (CAB) review process. Changes are submitted at least
72 hours before the desired deployment window and require CAB approval.

3.3 Emergency Changes During Active Incidents
During an active incident, emergency changes bypass the standard change advisory board
(CAB) review but must be retrospectively reviewed within 72 hours. The incident ticket
must be linked to the emergency change request in the change management system.

The following conditions must be met for an emergency change:
- An active P1 or P2 incident ticket must be open
- The change must be directly related to incident mitigation
- The engineer making the change must notify the incident commander
- All emergency changes must be documented with rationale and rollback procedure

Post-incident, the CAB must review all emergency changes made during the incident
window and formally close or remediate any that introduced new risk.

4. ESCALATION PROCEDURES

P1 incidents require immediate escalation to the on-call manager and service owner.
If the incident is not resolved within 2 hours, escalate to the VP of Engineering.
Customer-facing P1 incidents require a communication update every 30 minutes.

5. POST-INCIDENT REVIEW

All P1 and P2 incidents require a post-incident review document within 5 business days.
The review must cover timeline, root cause, contributing factors, and action items.
Action items must have owners and due dates assigned.

6. INCIDENT COMMUNICATION

Use the incident communication template in Confluence for all external communications.
All communications must be reviewed by the Customer Success team before sending.
