Data Governance and Retention Policy
Version 2.4 | Effective Date: 2024-03-15

1. PURPOSE

This policy establishes the standards for data classification, retention, storage
security, and disposal across all company systems. Compliance with this policy is
mandatory for all employees, contractors, and third-party processors.

2. DATA CLASSIFICATION

Data is classified into four categories: Public, Internal, Confidential, and Restricted.
Classification determines applicable retention periods, storage requirements, and
access controls. Data owners are responsible for correctly classifying data at creation.

3. RETENTION REQUIREMENTS

3.1 Customer Records
Customer records must be retained for a minimum of 7 years in accordance with
regulatory requirements. All customer records must be stored in encrypted format
using AES-256 or equivalent encryption standard approved by the security team.
Records exceeding the 7-year retention period must be securely disposed of using
NIST SP 800-88 compliant media sanitization procedures.

3.2 Financial Records
Financial records including invoices, contracts, and payment histories must be
retained for 10 years to satisfy tax authority requirements in all operating regions.

3.3 Employee Records
Employee records are retained for the duration of employment plus 7 years.
Payroll records are subject to statutory retention periods by jurisdiction.

3.4 Operational Logs
System access logs and audit trails must be retained for 2 years.
Security incident logs must be retained for 5 years.

4. STORAGE SECURITY

All retained data must be stored with encryption at rest and in transit. Encryption
keys must be managed through the approved key management service. Backup copies
must meet the same encryption standards as primary storage.

5. DISPOSAL

Data disposal must be documented and approved by the data owner and the data
protection team. Physical media must be physically destroyed. Digital records
must be securely wiped with verification.

6. POLICY VIOLATIONS

Violations of this policy may result in disciplinary action up to and including
termination. Regulatory violations may also result in civil or criminal penalties.
